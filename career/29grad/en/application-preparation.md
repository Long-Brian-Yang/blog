# Application Materials and Interview Preparation — Class of 2029

## 1. Core documents
- **Resume / CV:** Education, research areas, technical skills, presentations, publications, and activities. Verify facts and dates.
- **Research summary (one- and two-page versions):** Context, question, methods, novelty, results, significance, and possible industry relevance. Prepare a non-specialist version.
- **Application essays:** Motivation, strengths, research, challenges, collaboration. Tailor to the role and employer.
- **Portfolio:** Publicly shareable code, workflows, figures, and reproducibility. Do not disclose confidential or unpublished work.
- **Publications:** Clearly distinguish published, accepted, submitted, and in preparation.

## 2. Research pitch practice
| Length | Audience | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 30 seconds | Recruiter / new contact | Problem, why it matters, your contribution |
| 2 minutes | General technical audience | Objective, method, outcome, application |
| 5 minutes | Researchers | Computational setup, validation, limitations, next steps |

## 3. Selection preparation by role
- **Corporate R&D:** Technical interviews, research talks, originality, failures, reproducibility, experimental relevance.
- **AI / IT:** Coding, statistics/ML, algorithms, GitHub, engineering experience, depending on the position.
- **Consulting:** Cases, estimation, behavioral interviews, commercial reasoning; formats vary.
- **Generalist roles:** Motivation, collaboration, leadership, problem solving, business understanding.

## 4. Language preparation
- Japanese: Research introduction, motivation, interview register, thoughtful questions.
- English: Technical pitch, STAR behavioral stories, research Q&A, explaining work to teams.
- Verify actual language requirements in each official posting.

## 5. Questions to rehearse
Why pursue a PhD? What is novel in the research? What happened when results failed? How would you explain it to a non-specialist? Why industry rather than academia? Why this role? Where do you want to grow in five years? Describe a team disagreement.

## 6. Quality checklist
- [ ] Claims are supported by specific experience and evidence
- [ ] Non-specialists can understand the research significance
- [ ] Employer, position, and hiring year are accurate
- [ ] Confidentiality and publication permissions are respected
- [ ] Official Class of 2029 eligibility and deadlines are checked

**Progress:** Framework only. Personal achievements and answers must not be invented.
