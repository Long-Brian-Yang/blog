# Class of 2029: Integrated Interview Preparation Architecture

Updated 2026-10-27. Explore five career tracks without premature narrowing. Prioritize Tokyo. Do not invent individual achievements or motivations.

## One coherent narrative: five questions
1. WHO: Who am I? — Computational materials doctoral researcher interested in modeling and validation.
2. EVIDENCE: What have I done? — First-author ACS Omega manuscript under review, other research, teamwork and technical challenges.
3. MOTIVATION: Why industry? — Confirm actual personal reasons: application, impact, learning, etc.
4. FIT: Why this role and employer? — Connect verified skills to job requirements and company-specific work.
5. FUTURE: What will I contribute? — Early contributions and long-term growth.

| Module | Deliverable | To verify |
|---|---|---|

| Future and questions | Three/five-year direction and questions to employer | Role realities |

## Five separate evidence stories
1. ACS Omega first-author manuscript and peer review: persistence, rigor, response to scrutiny. **Not accepted as of 2026-10-27; over 20 new comments in round two.**
2. Independent research judgment: initiative and creativity (example unconfirmed).
3. Failure or unexpected result: diagnosis and improvement (unconfirmed).
4. Collaboration or communication: teamwork (unconfirmed).
5. Learning a new method: adaptability (unconfirmed).

## Research explanation at three depths
- 60 seconds: problem, significance, approach, finding.
- Five minutes: gap, method, personal role, findings, validation, limits.
- Fifteen minutes: DFT, AIMD, NNP, datasets, energy conservation, MSD, RDF, statistics, reproducibility, unresolved limitations.

## Role-specific focus
| Track | Emphasis | Extra preparation |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental R&D | Scientific question, method, evidence | Technical defense, research proposal |
| Applied R&D | Industrial relevance and experimental links | Products and business |
| AI/scientific computing | Implementation, evaluation, numerics | Coding and algorithms |
| Consulting/tech strategy | Problem framing and hypotheses | Cases and business literacy |
| Generalist | Initiative, learning, collaboration | Behavioral interviews |

## Core question bank
Tell me about yourself; explain your research to a non-expert; why this research; novelty and personal contribution; biggest challenge and response; current manuscript status; strengths and weaknesses; disagreements; why industry rather than academia; why AI/consulting/generalist; why this company; goals in three and five years; location preferences; questions for the interviewer.

## Consistency checks
- Every strength has a concrete example.
- Personal actions are explicit in the main story.
- Research claims match manuscript status.
- Employer motivation cites company-specific facts.
- Location/benefits preferences are distinguished from role motivation.
- Career pivots are not supported by exaggerated skills.

## Four-week sequence
Week 1: self-assessment, research inventory, five evidence stories.
Week 2: bilingual introductions, research summary, gakuchika, personal strengths.
Week 3: five role motivations, technical and case preparation.
Week 4: mock interviews, company-specific tailoring, revision.
