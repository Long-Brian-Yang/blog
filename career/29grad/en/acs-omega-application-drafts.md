# ACS Omega First-Author Paper: Class of 2029 Career Story
Updated 2026-10-27. **Under revision, not accepted.** The second review round involved three reviewers and more than 20 new comments. Do not invent personal contributions.

## Core behavioral interview story
One of the most demanding experiences of my doctoral studies has been preparing my first first-author manuscript and responding to multiple rounds of peer review.

My research investigates proton diffusion in Y-doped BaZrO3 using machine-learning interatomic potentials. I compare NEP, MACE, and SevenNet to evaluate not only predictive accuracy but also the reliability of long-time molecular dynamics simulations.

A central challenge is that obtaining computational results is different from demonstrating that those results are scientifically trustworthy. In the first review round, two reviewers raised substantive questions about training data, model accuracy, diffusion mechanisms, and statistical uncertainty. After revision, the second round involved three reviewers and more than 20 new comments, including fundamental concerns about energy conservation and the reliability of diffusion coefficients.

The process has reinforced the importance of evaluating computational work across several levels: model error, numerical stability, statistical variation, and physical consistency. It has also highlighted the value of engaging seriously with external criticism and reconsidering the assumptions supporting a conclusion.

As of October 2026, the manuscript is still under review. I do not present it as an accepted publication. What I can speak to is the ongoing experience of pursuing a demanding research goal while subjecting the evidence to increasingly rigorous scrutiny. It is shaping the kind of researcher I want to become: someone who takes responsibility not only for producing results but also for their reliability.
