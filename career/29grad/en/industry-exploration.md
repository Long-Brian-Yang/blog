# Industry and Role Exploration Guide

## Separate industry from job function
An industry describes what a company offers; a role describes the problems you solve. Materials companies hire AI specialists, and IT companies employ researchers.

| Path | Typical work | Link to research experience | Gaps to investigate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate labs / R&D | Fundamental research, applied research, patents, prototypes | Hypothesis testing, publications, simulation | Topic ownership, autonomy, business relevance, placement |
| Materials / chemicals / energy | Materials design, catalysts, batteries, processes | Atomic-scale modeling and physical understanding | Experimental and manufacturing interfaces, safety |
| IT / AI / computational science | ML, scientific computing, data platforms, software | Numerical modeling, programming, data | Software engineering, deployment, MLOps |
| Consulting / generalist | Problem framing, analysis, recommendations, operations | Structured reasoning, analysis, communication | Clients, business literacy, case interviews |

## Additional role families
Materials informatics, computational chemistry, CAE, simulation engineering, data science, technology strategy, intellectual property, technical sales, and product roles.

## Research sequence
1. Read real job descriptions and required skills.
2. Understand each company's research, products, and business strategy.
3. Ask employees about their actual work.
4. Test fit through internships, conversations, or small projects.
5. Compare interest, strengths, lifestyle, and opportunities.

## Informational interview questions
- What do doctoral hires work on initially and after 3–5 years?
- Who selects research topics and how?
- How do computational, experimental, and business teams collaborate?
- What outcomes matter: papers, patents, products, or revenue?
- How much Japanese and English is used?
- What should a prospective applicant prepare?
