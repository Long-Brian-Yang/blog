# 2026-10-26 — Why Is It So Hard to Say No?

> **Focus:** B2–C1 · Personal Boundaries · Social Psychology · Natural Spoken English  
> **Goal:** Set boundaries, decline requests politely, manage guilt, and express disagreement respectfully.

---

## 1. Reading

Have you ever agreed to do something even though you knew you didn't really want to? Maybe a friend asked you for a favor, a colleague needed help with an extra task, or someone invited you to an event when you were already exhausted.

For many people, saying no feels surprisingly uncomfortable. We worry about disappointing others, appearing selfish, or creating unnecessary tension. Sometimes, agreeing seems easier than explaining why we would rather not.

One reason is that we often confuse being kind with being available. We assume that if we genuinely care about someone, we should always be willing to help. But constantly putting other people's needs ahead of our own can gradually lead to frustration, exhaustion, and even resentment.

This is especially common among people who tend to seek approval from others. They may feel responsible for keeping everyone happy, even when doing so comes at the expense of their own well-being.

The challenge is learning that setting boundaries doesn't necessarily mean rejecting people. It simply means recognizing that our time, energy, and emotional capacity are limited. We can care about someone without agreeing to every request they make.

Of course, the way we say no matters. A blunt refusal might sound unnecessarily harsh, while an overly complicated explanation can make us appear uncertain about our own decision. Sometimes, a simple and respectful response is enough: "I'd love to help, but I don't have the capacity right now."

Learning to be assertive is not about becoming cold or inconsiderate. It's about expressing our needs honestly while respecting those of others. Healthy relationships should allow room for both generosity and personal boundaries.

Ultimately, saying no to one thing often means saying yes to something else: our priorities, our peace of mind, or the commitments we've already made. And perhaps the most important lesson is that we don't have to earn other people's respect by constantly making ourselves available.

## 2. 中文理解

你有没有过这样的经历：明明知道自己不想做某件事，却还是答应了别人？可能是朋友请你帮忙、同事希望你承担额外工作，或者你已经很累了，却还是接受了邀请。

对很多人来说，拒绝别人会带来不适感。我们担心让别人失望、显得自私，或者造成不必要的紧张关系。有时候，直接答应似乎比解释拒绝的原因更加容易。

其中一个原因是，我们经常把“善良”和“随时愿意帮助别人”混为一谈。我们可能认为，如果真正关心一个人，就应该始终愿意帮助对方。但不断把别人的需求放在自己之前，久而久之可能产生疲惫、挫败感，甚至怨气。

这种情况在习惯寻求他人认可的人身上尤其常见。他们可能觉得自己有责任让所有人开心，即使这意味着牺牲自己的身心状态。

**Setting boundaries ≠ Rejecting people.** 设定个人边界并不等于拒绝某个人。我们可以关心别人，却不必答应每一个请求。我们需要认识到自己的时间、精力和情绪承受能力都是有限的。

拒绝的方式也很重要。过于直接可能显得生硬，而解释过多又可能让决定显得不够坚定。有时，一句简单而礼貌的话就足够了：“I'd love to help, but I don't have the capacity right now.”（我很愿意帮忙，但目前确实没有余力。）

坚定表达并不等于冷漠或不体谅别人，而是在尊重他人的同时诚实表达自己的需求。健康的人际关系应该既允许慷慨付出，也允许保有个人边界。

最终，拒绝一件事往往意味着选择另一件更重要的事：自己的优先事项、内心的平静，或者已经承担的责任。我们不需要通过无条件满足别人的要求来赢得尊重。

### Usage Notes

- **assertive vs. aggressive:** Assertive 是坚定表达需求、同时尊重别人；aggressive 则可能带有攻击性。
- **capacity vs. ability:** Ability 强调是否有能力；capacity 也常强调目前有没有足够的时间、精力或资源。
- **I don't have the capacity right now** 是职场中委婉但清楚地说明目前无暇承担更多任务的表达。

### Grammar ① — Even though + clause

> I agreed to help even though I was exhausted.

尽管我已经很累了，还是答应帮忙。**Even though** 表示事实上的让步；**even if** 则常表示假设：“Even if I'm busy, I'll help her.”

### Grammar ② — Without + -ing

> We can care about someone without agreeing to every request.

我们可以关心别人，而不必答应每一个请求。

### Grammar ③ — Doesn't necessarily mean...

> Setting boundaries doesn't necessarily mean rejecting people.

设定边界不一定意味着拒绝别人。**necessarily** 强调两者并不存在必然联系。

### Grammar ④ — While + clause

> A blunt refusal might sound harsh, while a long explanation can make us appear uncertain.

直接拒绝可能显得生硬，而过长的解释又可能让我们显得不够坚定。

### Grammar ⑤ — The way + subject + verb

> The way we say no matters.

我们拒绝别人的方式很重要。

## 5. Natural Spoken English

> I've realized that / I sometimes have a hard time saying no to people.
>
> It's not that I always want to help. / I just don't want to disappoint anyone / or make things awkward.
>
> But the thing is, / when I keep agreeing to things I don't really want to do, / I end up feeling overwhelmed.
>
> So lately, / I've been trying to be more upfront about what I can and can't do.
>
> I think it's possible to be kind / without always putting yourself last.
>
> And honestly, / I'd rather say no politely / than say yes and regret it later.

**Speaking Tip — It's not that... It's just that...**

> It's not that I don't care. It's just that I need some time for myself.

并不是我不在乎，只是我需要一点自己的时间。适合解释容易被误解的真实想法。

### Mini Conversation

**Colleague:** Hey, could you help me finish this report tonight? I know it's last-minute.

**You:** I'd love to help, but I've already got a few deadlines to meet.

**Colleague:** It shouldn't take too long. Maybe just an hour?

**You:** I understand, but I really don't have the capacity tonight. I could take a quick look tomorrow morning, though.

**Colleague:** That would actually help a lot. Thanks!

**You:** No problem. Send it over, and I'll check it tomorrow.

**Communication Pattern:** Acknowledge → Set a boundary → Offer an alternative.

## 8. Speaking & Review

### Free Speaking

Answer for 2 minutes:

> **Do you find it difficult to say no to people, especially when you care about their feelings?**

讨论为什么不愿拒绝别人、是否曾因答应太多事情而疲惫，以及如何在体贴别人和维护个人边界之间取得平衡。

### One-Minute Version

> I think I sometimes have a hard time saying no, especially when I don't want to disappoint someone.
>
> For example, if a friend asks me for help when I'm already busy, I might agree even though I know I don't have enough time.
>
> But I've started to realize that constantly putting other people's needs first isn't always healthy.
>
> I'm trying to become more assertive and be honest about what I can and can't do.
>
> I think setting boundaries doesn't mean being selfish. It simply means respecting my own time and energy while still caring about others.
>
> And I'd rather be honest from the beginning than make a promise I might regret later.
