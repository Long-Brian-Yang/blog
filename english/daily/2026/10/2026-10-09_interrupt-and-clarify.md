# 2026-10-09 — How to Interrupt and Clarify Naturally

> **Focus:** natural conversation · clarification · meetings & social English  
> **Level:** B2–C1  
> **Goal:** interrupt politely, ask for clarification, and keep conversations flowing.

---

## 1. Reading

One of the most underrated communication skills is knowing how to interrupt someone without making the conversation feel awkward.

Many English learners hesitate to interrupt because they worry about sounding rude. As a result, they sometimes stay silent even when they have an important question or don't fully understand what the other person is saying.

But interrupting isn't necessarily impolite. In fact, asking a well-timed question can show that you're actively engaged in the conversation. The key is knowing when to jump in and how to phrase your interruption.

For example, instead of suddenly saying, "I don't understand," you might say, "Sorry to interrupt, but could you clarify what you mean by that?" This sounds more collaborative and gives the speaker an opportunity to explain their point.

Clarification is especially important in academic and professional discussions. People often use technical terms, make assumptions, or skip steps in their reasoning. If something isn't clear, it's usually better to ask than to pretend you understand.

Another useful technique is paraphrasing. You can briefly restate what someone has said and check whether your interpretation is correct. Expressions like "So, if I understand correctly..." or "Just to make sure we're on the same page..." help prevent misunderstandings before they become bigger problems.

Of course, timing matters. Constantly interrupting someone can make them feel that you're not listening. Sometimes it's better to let them finish their thought before asking a question.

Ultimately, good communication isn't about never interrupting. It's about knowing how to enter a conversation, clarify what matters, and make sure everyone understands each other.

## 2. 中文理解

一个经常被低估的沟通能力，是**如何在不让交流变得尴尬的情况下自然地打断别人**。

很多英语学习者担心打断别人会显得不礼貌，因此即使有重要的问题，或者没有完全理解对方的意思，也会选择保持沉默。

但打断别人并不一定是不礼貌的。恰当时机提出问题，反而能够说明你正在认真参与讨论。关键在于知道什么时候插话，以及如何组织自己的语言。

例如，与其突然说 *I don't understand*，不如说：

> Sorry to interrupt, but could you clarify what you mean by that?

在学术和工作交流中，这种能力尤其重要。人们经常使用专业术语、默认某些前提成立，或者省略推理过程中的部分步骤。与其假装理解，不如及时提出问题。

另一种非常实用的技巧是 **paraphrasing（转述）**：先用自己的话重新表达对方的意思，再确认自己是否理解正确。

当然，时机也很重要。不断打断别人会让对方觉得你没有认真倾听。有时候，等对方说完再提问会更合适。

**好的沟通不是从不打断，而是知道如何自然参与、澄清重点，并确保双方真正理解彼此。**

### Usage Notes

- **interrupt vs. disrupt:** *interrupt* 是中断讲话或活动；*disrupt* 通常表示对正常过程造成更明显的扰乱。
- **clarify vs. explain:** *explain* 是解释；*clarify* 特别强调把不清楚的部分说明白。
- **assume vs. presume:** 两者都表示假定，*presume* 通常更正式，有时暗示一定依据。

## 4. Key Chunks & Grammar

| Expression | 中文 | 使用场景 |
|---|---|---|
| **Sorry to interrupt, but...** | 不好意思打断一下…… | 礼貌插话 |
| **Can I jump in for a second?** | 我可以插一句吗？ | 会议、讨论 |
| **Could I just add something?** | 我能补充一点吗？ | 补充观点 |
| **Could you clarify what you mean by...?** | 你能澄清一下……是什么意思吗？ | 请求解释 |
| **What exactly do you mean by...?** | 你说的……具体指什么？ | 追问定义 |
| **So, if I understand correctly,...** | 所以，如果我理解得没错…… | 确认理解 |
| **Just to make sure we're on the same page,...** | 为了确保我们理解一致…… | 会议、合作 |
| **Let me make sure I've got this right.** | 我确认一下自己有没有理解正确。 | 复述确认 |
| **Could you elaborate on that?** | 能进一步展开说明吗？ | 深入追问 |
| **Before we move on,...** | 在进入下一个话题之前…… | 补问问题 |
| **Go ahead.** | 你先说。 | 礼貌让出话语权 |
| **Sorry, I didn't mean to cut you off.** | 不好意思，我不是故意打断你的。 | 意外打断 |

### Grammar Patterns

**Could you + verb?**

> Could you clarify what you mean by that?

比直接使用命令式更礼貌。

**What do you mean by + noun / -ing?**

> What do you mean by "model reliability"?

> What do you mean by saying the model is transferable?

**If I understand correctly,...**

> If I understand correctly, you're suggesting that we need additional validation.

**I was wondering if...**

> I was wondering if you could explain that part again.

更加委婉，但不必在所有日常场景中都使用。

## 5. Natural Spoken English

> Sorry, can I jump in for a second? /
>
> I just want to make sure / I'm following your point correctly.
>
> So, if I understand correctly, / you're saying that the model performs well on the test set, / but we haven't really checked how stable it is during long MD simulations.
>
> Is that right?
>
> Okay, that makes sense. / Could you elaborate a little on how you're planning to validate that?
>
> Sorry, one more quick question / before we move on.

**Speaking tip:** 一次礼貌的开场就足够了，不需要不断说 *Sorry*。清楚简洁地插话通常更自然。

## 6. Expression Upgrade

| Basic | More Natural |
|---|---|
| I don't understand. | **I'm not sure I'm following.** |
| Say it again. | **Could you go over that one more time?** |
| I have a question. | **Can I ask a quick question?** |
| What does that mean? | **What exactly do you mean by that?** |
| I want to say something. | **Could I just add something here?** |
| You mean this? | **So, if I understand correctly, you're saying...?** |
| Explain more. | **Could you elaborate on that a little?** |
| Wait, I want to talk. | **Sorry, can I jump in for a second?** |

### Research Example

**Professor:** We should evaluate the model's transferability before applying it to another system.

**You:** Sorry, could I clarify one thing? When you say transferability, do you mean evaluating the model on a completely different material, or on configurations that weren't included in the training set?

**Professor:** I mean both, but we should start with unseen configurations.

**You:** Got it. So, if I understand correctly, the first step is to evaluate how well the model generalizes beyond the training configurations.

## 7. Active Output

先自己说，再看答案。

1. 不好意思，我可以插一句吗？
2. 我不确定自己有没有完全理解你的意思。
3. 你能具体解释一下这里的 transferability 是什么意思吗？
4. 所以，如果我理解得没错，你的意思是我们还需要更多验证。
5. 在进入下一个话题之前，我想再问一个问题。
6. 你能进一步展开说明这个方法吗？
7. 不好意思，我不是故意打断你的，你继续说。

### Suggested Answers

1. **Sorry, can I jump in for a second?**
2. **I'm not sure I'm following your point completely.**
3. **Could you clarify what you mean by transferability here?**
4. **So, if I understand correctly, you're saying we still need more validation.**
5. **Before we move on, I'd like to ask one more question.**
6. **Could you elaborate a little on this approach?**
7. **Sorry, I didn't mean to cut you off. Please go ahead.**

## 8. Speaking & Review

### Mini Conversation

**Colleague:** I think we should retrain the model with a larger dataset.

**You:** Can I ask a quick question? What kind of additional data are you thinking of?

**Colleague:** Mainly structures from higher-temperature simulations.

**You:** Got it. So the idea is to improve the model's coverage of configurations that it might encounter during MD?

**Colleague:** Exactly.

**You:** That makes sense. Could I also suggest including some configurations with unusually large force errors?

### Free Speaking

Answer for 1–2 minutes:

> **Do you usually ask questions immediately when you don't understand something, or do you prefer to wait?**

Use: *jump in · clarify · I'm not sure I'm following · if I understand correctly · elaborate on*.

### One-Minute Version

> I think knowing how to interrupt politely is a really useful communication skill, especially in meetings.
>
> I used to think interrupting someone was always rude, but now I think it depends on the timing and the way you do it.
>
> If I'm not following someone's explanation, I can say something like, "Sorry, could you clarify what you mean by that?" Or I can repeat what I understood and check whether I've got it right.
>
> I think asking a good question is often much better than pretending you understand everything.
