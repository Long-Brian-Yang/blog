# 2025-01-31 — Interview Story: From Undergraduate Research to Master's Research

> **Focus:** STAR-style interview answer · self-directed learning · research growth

## 1. Reading — Undergraduate Research

During my undergraduate studies, I worked on CT medical-image segmentation. Because much of the research took place remotely during the COVID period, I had limited direct support and no strong background in deep learning at the beginning.

To catch up, I created my own learning plan. I studied through online courses, deep-learning materials, PyTorch tutorials, GitHub projects, and research papers. I then applied what I learned to preprocessing CT data, training segmentation models, and evaluating the results.

The experience strengthened my ability to learn independently and solve technical problems. It also gave me confidence that I could enter a new field even when I did not have all the necessary knowledge at the start.

## 2. Reading — Master's Research

For my master's research, I wanted to combine my machine-learning background with materials science. I began working on neural-network potentials and molecular-dynamics simulations for studying proton diffusion and material properties.

At the beginning, I spent several months building the necessary background in materials science and computational methods. I set monthly learning goals and used regular discussions and weekly seminars to check my progress.

Compared with my undergraduate research, I became more aware that good research is not only about independent problem-solving. It also requires collaboration, feedback, and the ability to adjust your plan when results do not develop as expected.

## 6. Natural Spoken English

My undergraduate research was actually my first serious experience with deep learning. I didn't have much background at the beginning, and because it was during COVID, a lot of the learning was self-directed.

So I basically built my own learning plan and learned from courses, PyTorch resources, GitHub projects, and papers. That experience gave me a lot of confidence in learning new technical areas on my own.

For my master's, I wanted to take that machine-learning background into materials science. That's how I got into neural-network potentials and molecular dynamics. The biggest difference is that I've learned to rely not only on independent learning but also on regular discussion and feedback.
